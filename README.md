# NBA Shooting Streak Simulation

## Project Overview

This sports analytics project examines whether shooting streaks in basketball can occur naturally through random variation or may provide evidence of a "hot hand."

Using Monte Carlo simulation, I modeled sequences of independent shots at different shooting probabilities and analyzed the distribution of longest made-shot streaks.

## Research Question

Is making five shots in a row strong evidence of a hot hand, or can streaks of that length occur frequently through chance alone?

## Methods

- Simulated 100-shot sequences
- Ran 20,000 simulations for each shooting probability
- Evaluated shooting probabilities of 35% and 45%
- Recorded the longest made-shot streak in each simulation
- Compared how frequently streaks of five or more occurred under the independent-shot model

## Key Findings

At a 35% shooting probability, the average longest streak was approximately 4.03 made shots, and a streak of at least five occurred in about 28.7% of simulations.

At a 45% shooting probability, the average longest streak was approximately 5.24 made shots, and a streak of at least five occurred in about 64.3% of simulations.

These results demonstrate that five-shot streaks can occur relatively frequently even when shots are modeled as independent, meaning that a streak alone is not necessarily strong evidence of a hot-hand effect.
